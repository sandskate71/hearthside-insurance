Short answer: barely.

Your homeowners policy treats your home as a home — not an office, a studio, or a workshop. The minute you're running a business out of it, two big gaps open up, and most people don't find them until they file a claim.

## The two gaps that catch people

**Business property is capped — low.** Most homeowners policies put a small sublimit on business equipment and inventory kept at home, often just a couple thousand dollars. If your spare bedroom holds $20,000 of recording gear, camera bodies, or product inventory, the policy may cover only a sliver of it.

**Business liability is usually excluded outright.** This is the bigger one. If a client, customer, or delivery driver gets hurt at your home for a business reason, your homeowners liability often won't respond — because it excludes business activities. And if your work involves giving advice or making something people use, professional and product liability aren't on a homeowners policy at all.

## The Nashville angle: the home-studio economy

Nashville runs on 1099 income. Producers and engineers with home studios, photographers, session players, Etsy and Whatnot sellers, consultants, contractors with a garage full of tools — a huge share of this city's businesses operate out of a house.

That's where we see the same gap over and over: thousands of dollars of equipment and real liability exposure sitting under a policy written for a family, not a business. People assume "it's at my house, so my house insurance covers it." Usually, not the way they think.

## Your options (and they're not one-size-fits-all)

The right fix depends on what you do:

- **A homeowners endorsement** — bumps up business property limits and adds limited liability. Fine for a low-risk side gig with no foot traffic.
- **An in-home business policy** — broader protection for a real operation run from home.
- **A Business Owner's Policy (BOP)** — combines property and liability for an established small business.
- **Standalone professional or general liability** — when advice, services, or customers on-site are part of the work.

## A 30-second gut check

Ask yourself three questions:

1. Do clients, customers, or vendors ever come to my home?
2. Do I keep valuable equipment or inventory here?
3. Do I give professional advice or make something people rely on?

A "yes" to any of these means your homeowners policy probably isn't enough on its own.

## The bottom line

A home-based business is still a business, and it deserves coverage that knows it. The good news: closing the gap is usually simpler and cheaper than people expect — once you know which option fits.
