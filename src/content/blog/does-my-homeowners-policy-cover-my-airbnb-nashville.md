Short answer: usually not the way you're hoping.

A standard homeowners policy is built around one idea — that you live in your home. The day you start renting it out to paying guests, even for a weekend, you've quietly stepped into business territory. And most homeowners policies were never designed to follow you there.

Nashville is one of the hottest short-term rental markets in the country, so we talk to a lot of homeowners who list a spare room or a whole house on Airbnb or VRBO and assume their regular policy has them covered. Often, it doesn't. Let's walk through it the way we would at the kitchen table.

## First, the part the city cares about: you need a permit

Before we even get to insurance, know this: in Nashville and Davidson County, you can't legally list a property for stays under 30 days without a Short-Term Rental Property (STRP) permit from Metro Codes. There are two permit types — owner-occupied and non-owner-occupied — and the permit has to be in hand before the listing goes live on any platform. It's good for one year and has to be renewed annually. The principal renter must be at least 21, occupancy is capped at twice the number of permitted sleeping rooms plus four, and you're responsible for collecting and remitting hotel occupancy and sales taxes.

Operating without a permit isn't a gray area in Nashville — it's illegal, and the fines escalate quickly with each violation.

That's the city's half. The insurance half is the one that quietly wrecks people, because nobody sends you a citation for it. You just find out at claim time.

## Why your homeowners policy may leave you exposed

Two gaps show up again and again:

**Business-use exclusions.** Once you're collecting money for stays, an insurer can treat that as a business activity. If a guest is injured, or a guest's carelessness starts a fire, your homeowners policy may limit or flat-out deny the claim because the loss came from a use the policy doesn't cover.

**Your stuff and your structure.** The furniture, linens, and appliances you bought specifically to host can be treated very differently once they're in service for paying guests rather than for your family.

## "But Airbnb covers me, right?"

This is the most common thing we hear. Platforms like Airbnb and VRBO do offer host protection, and it's better than nothing. But read the fine print: it's typically secondary coverage, it carries real exclusions, and it is not a substitute for a policy that actually fits how you're using the home. Leaning on platform coverage alone is like driving on a spare tire and calling it a car.

## What actually closes the gap

The right fix depends on how you host:

- **Occasional and owner-occupied** — you rent a room or the house a few weekends a year. Some carriers offer a home-sharing endorsement that extends your existing policy.
- **Regular or non-owner-occupied** — it's really an investment property. You're usually looking at a dedicated short-term rental or commercial policy built for transient guests.

The honest part nobody likes: the more you host, the less a standard homeowners policy fits, and the more it matters to have the right coverage in writing *before* something goes wrong — not after.

## The bottom line

A good policy should match how you actually live in — and rent out — your home. If you're hosting in Nashville and you've never told your insurer, there's a real chance you have a gap you can't see.
